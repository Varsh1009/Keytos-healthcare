MAE and r: cognitively normal people (out-of-fold). AUC: bias-corrected gap, dementia (CDR ≥ 0.5) vs normals at least as old as the youngest dementia participant, 1,000 bootstrap resamples. ICC and smallest detectable change: 20 real repeat scans. Coverage for single models uses their calibrated intervals (`cal_<model>`: residual SD by predicted-age band, scale fitted on training folds).

## What improved and what did not

- **Accuracy:** the lowest error is **Ensemble + volume model** at MAE 5.14 years, 2.21 years better than the baseline's 7.35.
- **CNN 2 mm:** MAE 5.95 (better by 1.40 years vs baseline); dementia AUC 0.711 vs 0.716.
- **SFCN fine-tuned (UK Biobank pretrained):** MAE 5.24 (better by 2.10 years vs baseline); dementia AUC 0.625 vs 0.716.
- **Reliability:** smallest detectable change ranges ±3.27 to ±5.73 years (baseline ±4.14; best Ensemble + volume model). All repeat scans are from 20 people aged 19–34, so this says nothing about reliability in older adults.
- **Intervals:** the ensemble's 95% intervals cover the true age of 94.0% of cognitively normal people (target 95%), mean width 24.1 years. Interval scale factor fitted on training folds: 0.81.
- **Confidence labels** (cognitively normal people): high 96 (MAE 3.16), medium 79 (MAE 5.62), low 141 (MAE 6.24).

## SFCN before fine-tuning (zero-shot check)

The untouched UK Biobank SFCN on our upsampled scans: MAE 8.36 years and r = 0.78 for the 122 normal people aged 44–80 (its training range); predictions spanned only 52–66 years across ages 18–94. The correlation shows the preprocessing (orientation, intensity) is right; the compressed range shows the domain shift (different scanner, 2 mm scans upsampled to 1 mm) that fine-tuning has to fix. Upsampling adds no detail.

## Confidence label rules (frozen)

- **High:** 95% interval narrower than 19.9 years, models agree within 10 years, template similarity ≥ 0.53 (5th percentile of training scans), age within 18–94.
- **Medium:** interval 19.9–28.4 years, or similarity between the upload cutoff 0.39 and 0.53.
- **Low:** interval wider than 28.4 years, models disagree by more than 10 years, age outside the training range, or similarity below the cutoff.
- How the cutoffs were set: spec cutoffs would label <5% of scans 'high', so cutoffs = terciles of 95% interval width on cognitively normal training-fold scans (labels are relative, not absolute accuracy).

## Deviations from the task spec (on purpose)

- Models are ensembled on **raw** predictions, not bias-corrected ones: bias-corrected brain age uses the person's real age and would make MAE look better than it is. The bias correction is applied to the gap afterwards, for every model in the same way.
- Bias-correction lines are fitted, for each fold, on the other folds' out-of-fold predictions (in-sample training predictions are overfit and would give the wrong line).
- Residual SD for the interval is looked up by **predicted**-age band, so an upload needs no age.
- The app ensemble uses image models only: the volume model needs OASIS's nWBV/eTIV, which an upload doesn't have.
- Left-right flips were off for the new models (spec); the baseline keeps its original flip augmentation.

## Limits

- Small training set: 316 cognitively normal people, one dataset (OASIS-1), one scanner type.
- Scans must be preprocessed like the training scans (skull-stripped, N4, FSL MNI152 2 mm).
- Test-retest reliability comes from 20 young adults only.
- A research tool, not a diagnosis.
